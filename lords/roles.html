Lord Roles & Secrets

Campaign relationships, hidden agendas, protected truths, and unresolved mysteries.

GM-ONLY MATERIAL

Using this section

These pages are intended for the Game Master. Secrets should enter play when the characters acquire meaningful evidence, access, witnesses, or campaign experience.

The Game Master's Guide is the reader-facing authority for Lord lore, history, portrayal, relationships, campaign context, and setting secrets. Lord mechanical statblocks remain in the Monster Compendium.

CAMPAIGN PRINCIPLES

Using Lords in Campaigns

  • Aion alone is the true god. Empyrean Lords are extraordinary Daevas dignified into Lordhood; Dragon Lords are extraordinary ascended Drakan/Balaur rulers.
  • Lords are people with convictions, loyalties, flaws, and relationships.
  • Lords are neither omniscient nor omnipresent. Investigation, institutions, agents, and PCs remain meaningful.
  • Direct Lord appearances should signal significance. Use agents, governors, commanders, temples, and orders for ordinary Lord influence.
  • A Lord should not solve the mission they gave the PCs or erase an unexpected player victory.
  • PCs may disagree with an Empyrean Lord. Lords can be wrong, misinformed, or strategically mistaken.
  • Dragon Lords are setting powers, not simply a higher Boss rank. Direct combat is exceptional; change the scenario objective rather than scaling one down for low-level PCs.
  • Permanent consequences from a Lord encounter are campaign-scale decisions, not automatic results of hit points.
  • Lord-centered campaigns keep PCs as protagonists. Victory can mean preventing an awakening, denying an Artifact, exposing a plan, forcing withdrawal, securing an alliance, or changing the balance.

PROTECTED HISTORY

Peace Conference & Cataclysm

Israphel's Secret Plan

Israphel proposed peace to break the Millennium War stalemate. Only he knew the proposal was a trap intended to bring the Dragon Lords inside the Great Barrier without their legions and eliminate them.

False Bargain

Israphel falsely promised Beritra that Empyreans and Dragon Lords would make peace, ascend together, and oppose Aion. Beritra believed the promise.

Fregion's Plan

Fregion independently intended to exploit access inside the Great Barrier to destroy the Tower and enable Balaur conquest.

Unidentified Attacker

An unidentified attacker attempted to assassinate Beritra. Beritra's lieutenant intercepted the fatal strike and died. The attacker's identity remains deliberately unresolved; Azphel is not canonically the attacker.

Siel's Sacrifice

The conference collapsed into Lord-level battle. Siel and Israphel defended the Tower. Siel sacrificed her essence to prevent complete annihilation and died permanently; her sacrifice ended ancient inherent Daeva resurrection.

Modern Knowledge

Ordinary modern Elyos and Asmodians do not know Israphel's secret plan or the false bargain with Beritra.

DRAGON LORD SECRETS

Present-Age Protected Truths

Apsu

Apsu is alive, severely weakened, hidden, and unknown to ordinary Elyos/Asmodian civilization. His later anti-Balaur conclusion and genuine willingness to cooperate are synchronized canon, but a future meeting is not mandatory.

Ereshkigal

Ereshkigal is alive and hibernating. Elyos and Asmodians do not know her current location. Her central sovereignty is the strategic order she established, not visible daily administration.

Fregion

Fregion remains alive. No default exact Present-Age location is invented unless the synchronized Lords source fixes it.

Meslamtaeda

Meslamtaeda remains alive within the locked chronology. No mandatory present appearance is required.

Beritra & Tiamat

Beritra and Tiamat remain actively at war. Later MMO outcomes are not baseline Wings of Atreia canon.

Dragon Lord Ascension

Aion ascended Fregion; Fregion discovered Id; Fregion ascended Meslamtaeda, Ereshkigal, Beritra, and Apsu, and later Tiamat. No future Dragon Lord outcome is predetermined after campaign start.

DELIBERATELY UNRESOLVED

Mysteries the GM May Develop

  • Identity of the person who attempted to assassinate Beritra.
  • Exact fate/current location of Israphel unless later fixed by explicit canon.
  • First inventor and development sequence of modern Aetheric Obelisks.
  • Deeper Rhoo, Lannok, Medeus/Nolantis, Besfer, and selected ancient-ruin mysteries.
  • Unmapped Greater Balaurea regions, peoples, routes, and histories not already fixed by Lord canon.

Campaign rule: A GM may define an unresolved mystery for their own campaign if the answer does not contradict established Lord canon, Core mechanics, or other Established GM Truth. Once adopted, that answer is campaign truth—not automatically official Wings of Atreia canon.

CAMPAIGN STRUCTURE

Lord-Centered Stories

A useful escalation path is legacy and rumors → subordinates → agents → direct consequences → audience or confrontation → possible Lord-scale resolution. This is guidance, not a mandatory ladder.

Existing Lord relationships should shape requests, rivalries, and strategy. Strong disagreement does not automatically mean constant lethal conflict. If PCs genuinely change a Lord's position, reflect political, religious, military, and faction consequences at the appropriate scale.

External MMO deaths, resurrections, and transformations are never automatic Wings of Atreia outcomes.
